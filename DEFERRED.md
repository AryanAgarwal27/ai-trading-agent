| D-6 | Stage 8g | The Redis kill subscription (`orchestrator/kill_subscription.py`, wired in `main.py` lifespan) writes `artifacts.kill_switch_event` into the matching thread's state, but nothing **wakes** a live thread in production to act on it: `schedule_wake` is paper-only (no live-wake job exists), and `/wake` only resumes `kind=="paper_wait"` (it 409s a `live_wait`). So a kill event sets state but the thread does not route to `live_pause` until a live-wake mechanism exists. 8g's tests drive wakes via `Command(resume=...)` directly, so the routing (`_route_after_live_wait` → `live_pause`, kill-switch payload discriminator) is proven; only the production *trigger* is absent. | Wiring a live-wake is a real design decision that 8g should not pre-empt: either (a) an APScheduler live-wake job mirroring paper's `schedule_wake` + extending `/wake` to accept a `live_wait` kind, or (b) having the kill subscription itself resume the thread immediately after writing the event (no scheduler round-trip). Picking one before it's designed bakes in the choice. **8h finding (`test_stage_8_e2e`): the subscription's parent-level `graph.aupdate_state` writing the kill event onto a thread parked at the NESTED `live_wait` interrupt CLEARS the parent-visible interrupt surface — `aget_state().tasks[*].interrupts` goes empty though the nested subgraph stays parked and resumes correctly via `Command(resume=...)`. This makes option (a) harder than it reads: the existing `/wake` (and `/approve`) endpoints gate on interrupt-presence and would 409 a kill-written thread, so reusing them requires loosening that guard — option (b) (direct resume from the subscription, bypassing the endpoint guard) sidesteps it.** **Operator-visibility consequence: in the window between the kill write and the wake, `GET /threads` reports `has_pending_interrupt=False` for the thread even though it IS parked, which pushes toward option (b) for a prompt, observable transition.** | A later sub-stage that introduces live-thread wake scheduling (8h or the Stage 9 supervisor). The owner wires either the live-wake job + `/wake` `live_wait` support, OR direct resume from the subscription, and removes D-6. |
